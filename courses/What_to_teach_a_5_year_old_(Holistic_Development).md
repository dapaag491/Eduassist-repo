## 🔹 Module 1: Social and Emotional Learning (SEL)
- **ID:** `node-1`
- **Progress:** [ ] Completed

**Description:**
Teaching your child how to identify emotions, regulate big feelings, develop empathy, and navigate social play (sharing, taking turns).

### 🔗 Resources
- [Social Development in Preschoolers: Learning How to Share & Cooperate](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Social-Development-in-Preschoolers.aspx) `[article]` - American Academy of Pediatrics guidance on preschoolers' growing awareness of others, sharing, cooperation, using words for feelings, and resolving everyday peer conflicts.
- [Milestones by 5 Years](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/5-years.html) `[article]` - CDC developmental milestones for age 5, including taking turns and following rules in games, participating in expressive activities, and completing simple chores.
- [Healthy Mental & Emotional Development: 4 Key Building Blocks](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/emotional-wellness/Building-Resilience/Pages/healthy-mental-and-emotional-development-in-children-key-building-blocks.aspx) `[article]` - Practical strategies for helping children identify, validate, and manage big emotions while maintaining kind, consistent behavioral limits.
- [Emotion Coaching: The Heart of Parenting](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v0vj9Z4kpEk) `[video]` - The Gottman Institute introduces emotion coaching: noticing emotions, listening with empathy, naming feelings, setting limits, and helping children solve problems.
- [Elmo Manages New Emotions with 'I Notice, I Feel, I Can'](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JwmnHOzytIU) `[video]` - A child-friendly Sesame Street video that teaches a simple three-step routine for noticing feelings and choosing a helpful response.
- [Social and Emotional Development in Kindergartners](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/preschool/Pages/Social-Emotional-Development-in-Preschoolers.aspx) `[article]` - An overview of what to expect emotionally from a 5-year-old and how to support their growing independence and complex feelings.
- [Emotion Coaching: One of the Most Important Parenting Practices](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JXNXaCSHpw) `[video]` - Dr. John Gottman's approach to validating children's emotions and guiding them through emotional regulation rather than dismissing them.

### 📖 Recommended Books
- **The Whole-Brain Child** by *Daniel J. Siegel & Tina Payne Bryson* - [Link](https://www.amazon.com/Whole-Brain-Child-Revolutionary-Strategies-Developing/dp/0553386697)
  > Offers 12 revolutionary strategies to nurture your child's developing mind, heavily focused on handling tantrums and teaching emotional regulation.
- **How to Talk So Kids Will Listen & Listen So Kids Will Talk** by *Adele Faber & Elaine Mazlish* - [Link](https://www.amazon.com/How-Talk-Kids-Will-Listen/dp/1451663889)
  > A classic parenting book offering practical, step-by-step methods for communicating effectively with children, encouraging cooperation, and resolving conflicts peacefully.

### 💡 Flashcards

| Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
| :--- | :--- |
| What is 'Name it to Tame it'? | A technique where you help a child label their emotion (e.g., 'You look frustrated'). Naming the emotion engages the logical brain and helps calm the emotional response. |
| How do you handle a child interrupting adult conversations? | Teach a physical cue, like placing their hand on your arm. You acknowledge them by placing your hand over theirs, finishing your sentence, and then turning your full attention to them. |

### ✏️ Practice Problems

#### 🔹 Emotion Charades
> Take turns acting out different emotions (happy, frustrated, scared, surprised) and having the other person guess. Discuss what your body feels like when you have that emotion.


#### 🔹 The 'Calm Down' Toolkit
> Work with your 5-year-old to create a 'calm down corner'. Have them pick 3-4 items (e.g., a squishy ball, a favorite book, a pinwheel for deep breathing) that they can use when they feel overwhelmed.


### ❓ Checkpoint Quiz

**1. Question 1**
- [ ] Explain logically why they cannot have the toy
- [ ] Send them to their room until they stop crying
- [ ] Validate their emotion and sit with them until they are calm
- [ ] Distract them with a different toy

**2. Question 2**
- [ ] Fully developed and consistent
- [ ] Still developing; they understand the concept but struggle with impulse control
- [ ] Non-existent; they cannot grasp the idea of others having things
- [ ] Only possible if an adult forces them to do it

## 🔹 Module 2: Foundational Academics: Literacy & Numeracy
- **ID:** `node-2`
- **Progress:** [ ] Completed

**Description:**
Building the building blocks of reading, writing, and math through play, curiosity, and environmental exposure rather than strict drills.

### 🔗 Resources
- [What 5-Year-Olds Learn in Kindergarten](https://www.pbs.org/parents/thrive/what-kids-learn-in-kindergarten) `[article]` - A breakdown of typical academic milestones for 5-year-olds, including phonemic awareness, counting, and pattern recognition.
- [Phonemic Awareness: How to Teach Reading](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=teaching+phonemic+awareness+to+kindergarten) `[video]` - Explains why hearing the sounds in words (phonemes) is more important for a 5-year-old than memorizing the alphabet song.

### 📖 Recommended Books
- **Teach Your Child to Read in 100 Easy Lessons** by *Siegfried Engelmann* - [Link](https://www.amazon.com/Teach-Your-Child-Read-Lessons/dp/0671631985)
  > A step-by-step, phonics-based program that requires just 20 minutes a day to teach foundational reading skills.

### 💡 Flashcards

| Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
| :--- | :--- |
| What is Phonemic Awareness? | The ability to hear, identify, and manipulate individual sounds (phonemes) in spoken words. Example: Knowing that 'cat' is made of the sounds /k/ /a/ /t/. |
| What is 'One-to-One Correspondence' in math? | The understanding that when counting objects, each object is counted once and only once. (e.g., Pointing to each block as they say '1, 2, 3'). |

### ✏️ Practice Problems

#### 🔹 Grocery Store Math
> At the store, ask your child to help you gather exactly 5 apples, 3 lemons, etc. Practice one-to-one counting by having them move each item from the shelf to the cart.


#### 🔹 Environmental Print Hunt
> Go for a walk and look for letters your child knows on stop signs, license plates, or store logos. 'Can you find the letter S? What sound does it make?'


### ❓ Checkpoint Quiz

**1. Question 1**
- [ ] Having them memorize flashcards of 100 sight words
- [ ] Reading aloud to them daily and pointing out rhyming words
- [ ] Making them write the alphabet 10 times a day
- [ ] Watching educational reading videos for two hours a day

## 🔹 Module 3: Practical Life Skills & Independence
- **ID:** `node-6`
- **Progress:** [ ] Completed

**Description:**
Encouraging responsibility through routines like organizing school materials, completing simple chores, managing personal hygiene, following multi-step instructions, and building executive functioning skills.

### 🔗 Resources
- [Age-Appropriate Chores for 5-Year-Olds](https://www.montessoriinreallife.com/home/chores-for-5-year-olds) `[article]` - A guide on how to involve young children in household maintenance to build their confidence and executive function.
- [Fostering Independence in 5-Year-Olds](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=montessori+practical+life+5+year+old) `[video]` - Visual examples of how to set up your home environment so a child can dress, feed, and clean up after themselves.

### 📖 Recommended Books
- **The Montessori Toddler (Concepts apply heavily to 5yo)** by *Simone Davies* - [Link](https://www.amazon.com/Montessori-Toddler-Parents-Raising-Responsible/dp/152350689X)
  > While titled for toddlers, the principles of setting up accessible environments and teaching practical life skills apply perfectly to preschoolers.

### 💡 Flashcards

| Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
| :--- | :--- |
| What is 'scaffolding' when teaching a life skill? | Breaking a task into smaller, manageable steps. First, you do it while they watch. Then, you do it together. Then, they do it while you watch. Finally, they do it alone. |
| Why are practical life skills (like pouring water or folding clothes) important for brain development? | They build executive function, concentration, fine motor skills, and a sense of belonging and capability within the family unit. |

### ✏️ Practice Problems

#### 🔹 The Morning Routine Chart
> Create a visual routine chart (using pictures, not just words) for the morning: Get dressed, brush teeth, eat breakfast, put shoes on. Have the child move a magnet or check off items as they complete them.


#### 🔹 Self-Serve Snack Station
> Organize a low drawer in the fridge or pantry with pre-portioned, healthy snacks. Teach the child the rule that they can choose one snack a day independently without asking.


### ❓ Checkpoint Quiz

**1. Question 1**
- [ ] Cooking dinner on the stove
- [ ] Sorting laundry by color and matching socks
- [ ] Mowing the lawn
- [ ] Being entirely responsible for feeding the dog without reminders

## 🔹 Module 4: Physical Development: Gross & Fine Motor Skills
- **ID:** `node-4`
- **Progress:** [ ] Completed

**Description:**
Developing the physical strength, coordination, and dexterity required for everything from climbing and jumping to writing and cutting.

### 🔗 Resources
- [Fine Motor Skills Development for Kindergarteners](https://theimaginationtree.com/40-fine-motor-skills-activities-for-kids/) `[article]` - Fun, play-based activities to strengthen hand muscles in preparation for writing.

### 💡 Flashcards

| Front (Question) | Back (Answer) |
| :--- | :--- |
| What is the 'Tripod Grasp'? | The ideal pencil grip for writing, using the thumb, index, and middle fingers. 5-year-olds should be transitioning to this grip. |
| How do gross motor skills impact fine motor skills? | Core and shoulder strength (gross motor) provide the stability needed for the hand and fingers (fine motor) to perform precise tasks like writing. |

### ✏️ Practice Problems

#### 🔹 Tweezer Rescue
> Drop small items (like beads or dried beans) into a bowl of water or kinetic sand. Give your child a pair of tweezers or child-safe tongs to 'rescue' them and transfer them to a dry bowl.


#### 🔹 Living Room Obstacle Course
> Build an obstacle course requiring them to balance on a line of tape, crawl under a table, hop on one foot between pillows, and throw a rolled-up sock into a laundry basket.


### ❓ Checkpoint Quiz

**1. Question 1**
- [ ] Force them to trace letters with a pencil for 30 minutes a day
- [ ] Have them play with playdough, use tweezers to pick up small objects, and practice cutting with scissors
- [ ] Buy a thicker pencil
- [ ] Wait a year; it happens naturally
